03 — Problem & opportunity

The category is bifurcated. That’s the opening.

The problem operators feel

  • Printed menus are static, expensive to reprint, and say nothing about what sells.
  • QR-menu SaaS (MENU TIGER, GloriaFood) is cheap but thin — no search, no dietary filters, no recommendations, weak loyalty.
  • Full POS suites (Petpooja, Toast, Square) are powerful but utilitarian, and meter you monthly forever.
  • Nobody pairs a cinematic guest experience with deep operations at a one-time price.

Why now

  • QR ordering is now a guest expectation, not a novelty — post-pandemic behaviour stuck.
  • UPI + India’s 2026 dining boom make pay-at-table frictionless.
  • AI video generation collapsed the cost of a cinematic menu from studio-shoot money to a few hundred rupees a clip.
  • GloriaFood (Oracle-owned) is discontinued to new signups — a free-tier vacuum opening across global markets.
The winner isn’t the cheapest QR code or the deepest POS. It’s the product that makes the guest feel something and gives the owner numbers they trust.

06 — The guest journey

Scan → savour → settle.

Browse a living menu

Category tabs, hero video per section, dish cards with price, spice level and dietary badges. Tutorial: guests tap a category to page through it, tap any dish for the full detail sheet. The QR carries the table id (?t=T07), so every order knows where it’s going.

Search & dietary filters

A fuzzy search with vegan / Jain / gluten-free / nut-free toggles and a spice slider — structured dietary & allergen data MENU TIGER and most QR rivals don’t carry. Tutorial: tap the search icon, type or filter, results group by category.

Item detail & modifiers

Full-bleed dish video, description, spice flames, allergen disclosure and priced modifier groups (crust, toppings, scoops) with live price. Tutorial: choose options, watch the price update, “Add to Order.”

Ask the AI

A short mood / party-size / budget flow returns curated dishes and bundles them into a money-saving combo. Tutorial: answer two taps, add the suggested combo in one tap.

The cart, live to the kitchen

Quantities, per-item notes, running total. “Place Order” fires the ticket straight to the kitchen display; the Dynamic Island keeps the cart one tap away. The cart now survives a refresh.

Pay-at-table — the money loop

Subtotal → 5% service → CGST/SGST → tip presets → split 1–4 ways → pay (UPI/cards via Razorpay, or a keyless demo). A checkout upsell offers “something sweet.” Tutorial: tap Pay bill, pick a tip and split, tap Pay — then you land in the review flow.

New  Reuses the same exact-sum split & GST engines the waiter bill uses, so guest and staff totals always match.

Post-pay review (the Sunday pattern). 4–5★ routes to a public Google review; 1–3★ stays private for the manager. Turns a paid bill into reputation.
Loyalty in the same flow. Link by phone, earn points per spend, see tier and visits. No separate app, no card.

Kiosk mode

Append ?kiosk=1 and the same menu becomes a full-screen self-order station (the Flipdish pattern) — counter-service and QSR ready, no extra build.

▶ Pitch it

“From scan to paid, the guest never waits for a server and never installs anything — and you get an upsell and a review on every visit.”

Key insight: walk a real order live on your phone while you talk. The checkout (₹120 → ₹132 with tip/split) and the post-pay review are the two ‘wow’ beats — slow down there.

The moat

  • Craft. The cinematic, themeable, motion-rich front-end is genuinely hard to copy and impossible to match on a budget SaaS P&L.
  • Video as margin engine. AI generation turns a once-₹lakh shoot into a few hundred rupees, baked into a premium tier.

Platform cost

Fixed, not per-seat.

The whole platform rides on a fixed ~₹2,580/mo infrastructure base (edge hosting + Supabase), with video egress the only variable. Each new venue adds rupees, not hundreds — so gross margin climbs with scale.
